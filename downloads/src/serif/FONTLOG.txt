FONTLOG for Vigil Serif
-------------------------------------------------------------------
This file provides detailed information on the Vigil Serif font software and
should be distributed along with the Vigil Serif fonts and any derivative works.

Vigil Serif is a derivative of Vollkorn by Friedrich Althausen, reproportioned
to pair with Vigil, a softened geometric grotesk, so that the two share a cap
height, an x-height and a line box. Vollkorn's own information and its changelog
from 2005 onward follow this section unchanged, as the SIL Open Font License FAQ
asks of a derivative.

Vollkorn declares no Reserved Font Name, so the rename is permitted outright.
The licence requires only that Vollkorn's copyright notice and the licence
travel with every copy, which they do: in OFL.txt, in the copyright field of
every font file, and on the specimen page.

vigilfont.com

(Most recent first. Vollkorn's own entries continue below this section.)

20 September 2026 (Joaquin Keno Vigil) Vigil Serif Version 1.300
- Added the eight hooked letters Fulah and Hausa need: U+0253 U+0257 U+0199
  U+01B4 and their capitals U+0181 U+018A U+0198 U+01B3. They were the last
  of the seventeen glyphs fontbakery's Google Fonts profile wanted and the
  one check it still failed; all seven West African orthographies now set,
  and the profile passes with no failing check (0 FAIL, 242 PASS). It still
  warns that the hooked letters have no small capitals, as the open letters
  of 1.200 have none: those twelve small caps are the next drawing.
- The drawing is two part glyphs per master, hook.comp for the lowercase and
  hookcap.comp for the capitals, in the font's own part-glyph convention
  (schulter.comp, bauch.comp). Each letter is its base letter's own contours
  with the top serif trimmed off the stem and the part placed on it, so the
  letters overlap the way the rest of the font overlaps rather than being
  merged. Drawn on commission by Claude (Anthropic), from the brief in
  DESIGN-BRIEF.md item 15; tools/draw_hooks.py is the drawing and rebuilds
  the ten glyphs from the base letters.
- The lowercase hook is a wide, shallow arch measured from Times New Roman
  and Baskerville -- the same size in the bold as in the regular, only the
  stroke following the weight -- ending the way this font ends a stroke, on
  a chopped beak rather than a ball. The capital hook curls left: the top
  serif turned into a hook, a slab continuing the letter's top edge past the
  stem and turning down into a beak. Ɓ and Ɗ curl left in every text serif
  that carries them, and must, because B and D have their top arm where a
  right-curling hook would land. Ƙ and Ƴ take the same part, Ƴ on the left
  arm. ƴ carries the arm's serif flare up as a short stem to a hook cresting
  at the ascender, as in Times. Nothing is raised: every hooked letter tops
  out where its base letter does, except ƴ, which reaches the ascender.
- Spacing is measured from the font's own letters. ɓ and ƙ keep their stems
  where b's and k's are. ɗ and ƴ, whose hooks overhang to the right, take the
  clearance the serif had on that side, which keeps the hook off a following
  ascender or dot, and are kerned back to the base letter's advance against
  the letters that stay under the hook -- decided geometrically per master,
  with a margin. The capitals move right so the hook's tip gets the clearance
  the serif's tip had. Each hooked letter joins its base letter's kerning
  group on the side that is unchanged and none on the hook side.
- Light and ExtraLight take the ten glyphs by the same arithmetic that
  generates those masters, applied to the ten alone so nothing already
  released moves; the Y's junction repair is carried into Ƴ and the italic
  K's into Ƙ. fontTools' interpolation check is silent on both designspaces.

19 September 2026 (Joaquin Keno Vigil) Vigil Serif Version 1.200
- Added the nine Latin glyphs standing between the font and seven African
  orthographies. fontbakery's Google Fonts profile puts the font in the
  GF_Phonetics_SinoExt glyphset on the strength of its existing Latin Extended
  coverage and then fails it for mandatory orthography codepoints it does not
  have; seventeen were missing. Nine are now in, and five of the seven languages
  pass: Yoruba, Bambara, Dyula, Fanti and Akuapem Twi.
- Five of the nine needed no drawing: m and M with acute (U+1E3F, U+1E3E) and n
  and N with grave (U+01F9, U+01F8) are composites placed from each master's own
  anchors rather than by copying a neighbour's baked offset, and U+0329, the
  combining vertical line below, is the master's own verticallinemod scaled into
  the band the other below-marks occupy -- top-aligned with dotbelowcomb at 1.55
  times its depth -- so every master derives it from its own outline and the
  point structure stays identical for interpolation.
- Two more needed none either, which is worth recording because it is not
  visible from the character set. U+025B, the open e, is epsilon, already drawn.
  U+0190, its capital, is Reversedze-cy (U+0510), and that is a real capital in
  its own right: its own 52-point outline standing -7 to 709, drawn at cap height
  and at capital weight, with nothing in the Latin cmap pointing at it. The
  alternative had been to scale epsilon by cap over x-height, 1.40, and then walk
  the weight back down a Regular-to-Black blend to undo the 40% the scale adds to
  every stroke -- a judgement call at every master, replaced by a drawing the
  designer already made. Both are plain components and track their base across
  the axis.
- The open o, U+0254 and U+0186, is c and C reflected. Reflecting is honest here
  only because Vollkorn's stress is vertical: o's thin points sit 8 units left of
  centre on a 482-unit width, 1.7%, so the flip does not visibly reverse the
  stress axis the way it would in a face with real diagonal stress. The italics
  are reflected and then sheared by twice the 11 degree italic angle, since a
  mirror about a vertical axis reverses the lean.
- Their spacing is measured rather than reasoned about, because reasoning gave
  the wrong answer twice. Swapping the sidebearings in the deslanted frame is
  exactly right for the romans and leaves the italic open o overlapping its
  neighbour by 30 units; swapping them in the slanted frame is closer but misses
  by -16, +4, -30 and +8 across the four masters. So tools/add_open_letters.py
  compares the closest ink approach in o + open-o against c + o, and in
  open-o + o against o + c, and moves the glyph until they agree. The romans
  settle after 0 to 4 units, which is the evidence that the measurement is sound;
  the italics move about 95 units left, with the advance almost unchanged.
- All four open letters joined the kern classes their shape earns, or they would
  have been the only letters in the font with no kerning at all. The open e and
  its capital take the classes their own base already sits in; the reflections
  take the right-hand class a bowl earns, as ereversed and schwa already do. The
  reflections' left-hand class is deliberately left unset: their left side is a
  pair of open terminals, which no lowercase in this font has, and inheriting c's
  public.kern2.o would hand them the tuck T and k give a round left side, when
  terminals reach further out than a bowl does.
- The copyright notice in OFL.txt and in every font file now opens in the form
  Google Fonts asks for, naming the Vigil Serif Project Authors and the
  repository. Vollkorn's notice is untouched and still travels with it, in the
  same string and in the same file, as the licence requires.
- Set nameID 25 on the italic variable font, the Variations PostScript Name
  Prefix. macOS names each instance from it rather than from nameID 6, so
  without one the italic's eight instances derive the same PostScript names as
  the roman's and the two families collide in the system font list.
  tools/finish_build.py now sets it, and only on italics, which is the only
  place it is asked for. With that and the copyright form above, the Google
  Fonts profile has one failing check left -- the eight hooked letters -- and
  242 passing.
- Still missing, and the reason Fulah and Hausa still fail: the eight hooked
  letters, b d k y with hooks, upper and lower. Those are real drawing -- the
  font owns no top-of-stem hook to borrow, N with left hook curling from the
  bottom left and f with hook curling the wrong way -- and are left for a later
  release.

18 September 2026 (Joaquin Keno Vigil) Vigil Serif Version 1.100
- Added an ExtraLight at 200, roman and italic, so the axis runs 200 to 900 and
  the serif reaches the light end the sans already had. Sixteen static instances.
  It is built the way the Light was and from the same two drawn masters, at twice
  the step: Regular + 0.4 * (Regular - Black), which lands on design 64. Measured
  on the build, the stem comes out at 55 against the Regular's 98 and the Light's
  76, and the contrast (the stem over the o's thinnest) eases from 1.95 at Regular
  through 1.69 at Light to 1.39, which is the direction a lighter cut wants: its
  thins proportionally thicker, or they disappear.
- Rejoined the junctions the second extrapolation pulled apart. Vollkorn draws
  many letters as overlapping pieces rather than one merged outline, and every
  step lighter spends some of the overlap; at 200 thirty of them had gone
  negative and 113 encoded glyphs rendered in two halves. E and every accented
  form that composites from it, Y and the yen, F and the franc, f, long s, x,
  chi, kappa, the ampersand, the section sign, all four rays of the currency
  sign, the ff fk and long-s-k ligatures, the small-cap f, and in the italic
  H K AE and their Cyrillic cousins. tools/repair_extralight_junctions.py moves
  89 points across 45 junctions; every edge it moves is inside the piece that
  laps over it, so no letter changed shape, only the depth of an overlap nobody
  can see. The Y needed more than that: its fork's flat bottom had risen to
  within half a unit of the notch between the arms, so the arms met along a
  hairline. Both bottom corners now run on down their own edges into the stem.
- Repaired sixteen junctions in the Light masters at the same time. Measuring
  every pair of contours in every glyph in order to size the 200's repairs found
  nine that were already severed at 300 in 1.000 -- the roman E and reversed E,
  both chis, the ff ligature's second f, the fk and long-s-k ligatures, the
  small-cap f -- and six more holding by less than a unit. They rendered joined
  only because the rasteriser filled the last fraction, which is luck, not
  drawing. Same kind of edit, same invisibility: no Light outline moved.
- Known at 200 and left alone on purpose: the italic ordfeminine, whose shoulder
  notch the extrapolation deepens (a redraw, not an edge to hide); the italic
  tcaron, whose caron sits clear of the t as it already did at 300; H with breve
  below, whose mark stops touching the H, which is the more correct placement;
  and three bracket-layer glyphs that are only ever substituted above 700.

18 September 2026 (Joaquin Keno Vigil) Vigil Serif Version 1.000
- First release, as Vigil Serif: the name is final. Variable roman and italic
  on one weight axis from 300 to 900, and fourteen static instances.
- The statics are hinted with ttfautohint and carry the hinted flag; the
  variable fonts carry a gasp table and a smart-dropout prep program. Every
  font has a STAT table with the seven named weights and an ital axis linking
  roman to italic, so the family enumerates as one on Windows and macOS.
  Every instance names its PostScript font; usWinAscent/Descent are the
  family's real bounds.
- The Light, extrapolated from the Regular-Black axis, was measured stroke by
  stroke against the Regular along the normals of the union outline: nothing
  thins beyond proportion, so it ships as drawn. Small caps keep Vollkorn's
  ratio to the cap height.

18 September 2026 (Joaquin Keno Vigil) — pre-release work, version 0.001
- Reconnected the figure features to the figures that are actually encoded.
  Making lining figures the default moved the codepoints, which silently killed
  sups, subs, numr, dnom and ordn, and half of frac, because all of them were
  written against the bare digit names. 504 rules added across the six feature
  files; no outline moved and no glyph was renamed.
- Rejoined the junctions the Light extrapolation pulled apart. Vollkorn draws
  several letters as overlapping pieces, and extrapolating below Regular shrank
  the overlap until it went negative, so nineteen glyphs rendered in two halves
  at weight 300: f and longs, Y and the fifteen that composite from it, F and
  franc. Four hidden edges moved back inside the pieces that lap over them, so
  no visible outline changed and Regular came out byte-identical.
- Cleared the stale upstream files that still described Vollkorn rather than
  this font, and renamed the designspace source and instance labels. The
  copyright notice, this FONTLOG and the attribution on the specimen page are
  kept deliberately.

16 September 2026 (Joaquin Keno Vigil)
- Repaired the case map. The reproportioning scripts chose a glyph's scale from
  the Unicode category of its codepoint and fell back to the cap factor for
  anything unencoded, so every unencoded lowercase glyph grew about 5% too
  little: the italic bowls and shoulders, every drawn ligature, and the
  stylistic and locl alternates.

15 September 2026 (Joaquin Keno Vigil)
- Reproportioned to Vigil's metrics: cap and x-height raised, the lowercase

  to cap height.
- Lining figures made the default, old-style moved to onum.
- A Light master added at 300 by extending the Regular to Black axis, so the

Basic Font Information
–––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––
Vollkorn is a quiet, modest and well working body copy typeface for bread and butter use. It has dark and meaty serifs and a bouncing and healthy look and might be used as body type as well as for headlines or titles. More than 2000 glyphs per font support a wide range of languages in Latin, Cyrillic and Greek scripts.
»Vollkorn« [pronounce ‘follkorn’] is German for “wholemeal”. It refers to the old term “Brotschrift” [literally ‘bread type’] which described the small fonts for every day use in the days of hand-compositing.
Vollkorn came into being as Friedrich Althausen’s first type designing attempt while his studies at Bauhaus University Weimar, Germany. The Regular style was initially published in 2005 under a Creative-Commons-License. When GoogleFonts launched in 2010 Vollkorn was one the first twenty featured fonts.
Since 19 May 2010 Vollkorn is published under the OFL.
Original Designers address: Friedrich Althausen, friedrichalthausen.de, Germany
